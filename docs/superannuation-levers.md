# Superannuation as a tax instrument

*English · [中文](zh/superannuation-levers.md)*

Super is usually filed under "retirement", which is why its tax levers get missed at return time. Several of them are decided — or lost — in the year you lodge, not decades later.

## 1. The concessional contribution arbitrage

A personal deductible contribution is taxed at 15% going into the fund instead of your marginal rate. For someone in a high bracket, that gap is the largest routine tax lever available to an employee.

```text
value of the arbitrage = (marginal rate including Medicare) − 15%
```

Two things are commonly misunderstood:

**It is a redirection, not a discount.** The money goes into a preserved account you cannot touch until preservation age. The benefit is real, but it is bought with liquidity, not found for free. Someone with no emergency reserve is usually worse off locking money away — a forced reliance on credit at 20% can wipe out a 24% saving in months.

**It does not reduce income for surcharge purposes.** Personal deductible contributions are reportable superannuation contributions and are added back for the income tests that drive the Medicare levy surcharge, the private health insurance rebate tier and study loan repayments. Only the income tax itself falls.

## 2. Carry-forward: the cap you did not use is an asset with an expiry date

Where total super balance at 30 June of the previous year is under the threshold, unused concessional cap from the previous five years can be used in the current year. Unused amounts expire after five years, oldest first.

This matters most for people with interrupted or low-income years — study, a career break, part-time work, early-career research. Those years quietly build a large carry-forward balance.

Two practical points:

**The pool is often self-sustaining.** If annual employer contributions sit well below the cap, a similar amount of new headroom accrues each year as the oldest tranche expires. Losing one year's tranche is not a catastrophe; the pool stays roughly stable. Check this before treating an expiry as urgent.

**The balance is published.** The tax authority's online services show the accumulated unused cap and the year-by-year breakdown. Read it rather than estimating — and note it also shows whether the eligibility test is met.

## 3. Balance between spouses is a structural decision

Two limits make this matter, and both are per person, not per couple:

| Limit | Why concentrating in one name is costly |
| --- | --- |
| **Transfer balance cap** — the ceiling on what can be moved into a tax-free retirement income stream | Unused cap in the lower-balance spouse's name cannot be transferred or shared. It is simply lost. |
| **Large-balance surcharge** — an additional tax on earnings attributable to a balance above a threshold | Two balances below the threshold are taxed less than one balance above it plus one well below. |

A couple whose contributions run 2:1 for twenty years ends with a split that wastes a large part of one cap and pushes the other toward the surcharge. The fix — contribution splitting, directing voluntary contributions to the lower balance, spouse contributions — is cheap early and expensive late.

**Model the split before directing a large voluntary contribution stream.** Where it goes is usually worth more than how much it is.

## 4. Insurance inside super: check the occupational rating

Default cover is priced for an unspecified occupation, which is the most expensive assumption the insurer can make. Most funds let a member state their occupation and move to a cheaper rating — office-based work almost always qualifies for a better one.

```text
check: what occupational rating does the account currently show?
```

If the answer is "default", the premium is being set by an assumption, not by the job. On a mid-size balance the difference can be several hundred dollars a year, which compounds against the balance for decades.

A caution and a surprise:

**Applying a rating is a declaration to an insurer.** Answer from the facts of the role, not from what produces the cheapest outcome. A misstatement can be corrected at claim time, which is the worst possible moment.

**Test whether the marginal question actually changes the price.** Funds commonly reach the cheapest rating through more than one route — for example on qualification and income alone, without needing a management-role question answered yes. Where the price is identical either way, answer the awkward question honestly and lose nothing.

## 5. What this means at return time

- Personal deductible contributions need a **notice of intent lodged with the fund and acknowledged before the return is lodged** — this is the most common way the deduction is lost.
- Employer contributions reported late (defined benefit funds, self-managed funds) can change the carry-forward figure after year end. Check the balance again before relying on it.
- The contribution must be **received by the fund** before 30 June, not merely sent.

## 6. What to record each year

- Total super balance at 30 June for each person, and the gap between them;
- Accumulated unused concessional cap and the year each tranche expires;
- The occupational rating on each insured account, and the premium;
- Insurance cover types and amounts, so the trajectory is visible — cover usually needs to be high while the balance is small and can taper as self-insurance builds.
